I am a slice of a call stack, the contexts that a capture cut off the stack down to a delimiter of my #prompt, a DelimccPrompt. The capture cuts my bottom context off its sender, so I do not hold the stack below the delimiter. Use me like a block with #value or #value:, or with #pushSubCont:, which run my contexts on top of the current call stack. I can be invoked many times.

The first invocation runs my own contexts, the ones that the capture cut off the stack, so it is exactly the computation that the capture interrupted: closures keep their homes, exceptions their handlers. Before it runs them, it keeps a DelimccSliceTemplate of them, made by DelimccSliceCopier, and drops them. Each later invocation runs fresh copies from the template, in which the closures, the exceptions, the temp vectors and the contexts of the slice refer to the copies of each other, not to the contexts of another invocation: a ^ in a block of the slice returns from the copy of its home, and a handler of the slice handles, resumes, retries and returns in the copy.

So the temporaries of my contexts are copied with them at each invocation, as they were at the first one, whether the compiler kept them in the contexts or in temp vectors; only heap objects (instance variables, collections, a ValueHolder, a dynamic variable) are shared by the invocations, as the heap is in Kiselyov's delimcc. A closure that a heap object holds is not re-pointed, so a ^ in it works in the first invocation only.

A capture runs no unwind block, and neither does a continuation that is never invoked; each invocation that completes or is unwound runs the ensure: and ifCurtailed: blocks of its own contexts once, as delimcc does.
